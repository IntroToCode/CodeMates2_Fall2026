# Idli

## Description

Idli is a traditional South Indian breakfast made from a fermented batter of rice and urad dal. The batter is steamed in small molds, creating soft, fluffy, and lightly tangy cakes that are typically served with sambar and chutney.

### Ingredients:

- [ ] 4 cups idli rice or parboiled rice
- [ ] 2 cups whole urad dal, skinned
- [ ] 2 teaspoons fenugreek seeds
- [ ] 2–4 teaspoons salt, or to taste
- [ ] Water, as needed for soaking and grinding
- [ ] Oil or ghee, for lightly greasing the idli molds

### Steps for preparation:

1. Rinse the rice several times until the water runs mostly clear. Place it in a large bowl, cover with plenty of water, and soak for 4–6 hours.
2. Rinse the urad dal and fenugreek seeds, then soak them together in a separate bowl for 4–6 hours.
3. Drain the urad dal and fenugreek seeds. Grind them with small amounts of water until the mixture is very smooth, light, and fluffy. Work in batches if necessary.
4. Drain the rice and grind it separately with enough water to form a slightly coarse but pourable batter. Work in batches if necessary.
5. Combine the ground rice and urad dal mixtures in a large bowl with enough room for the batter to rise. Mix thoroughly until evenly combined.
6. Cover the bowl loosely and leave the batter in a warm place for 8–12 hours, or overnight, until it has risen and looks bubbly.
7. Once fermented, gently stir the batter and add salt. If the batter is too thick, add a small amount of water. It should be thick but easy to spoon or pour.
8. Add water to the bottom of an idli steamer or large pot and bring it to a boil.
9. Lightly grease the idli molds with oil or ghee.
10. Spoon the batter into each mold, filling it almost to the top.
11. Place the idli stand inside the steamer, cover, and steam for about 10–12 minutes.
12. Turn off the heat and let the idlis rest for 2–3 minutes before opening the steamer.
13. Insert a toothpick or knife into an idli. If it comes out clean, the idlis are cooked.
14. Use a wet spoon or small spatula to gently remove the idlis from the molds.
15. Repeat the steaming process in batches until all the batter is used.
16. Serve warm with coconut chutney, tomato chutney, sambar, or podi.

### Notes:

- Fermentation time depends on the temperature of your kitchen. In colder weather, the batter may need longer than 12 hours.
- Use a sufficiently large bowl for fermentation, as the doubled quantity of batter will expand significantly.
- Grind the urad dal until very fluffy to help create soft idlis.
- Avoid stirring the fermented batter too aggressively so you preserve the air created during fermentation.
- The larger quantity may require multiple steaming batches, depending on the capacity of your idli steamer.
- Leftover batter can be refrigerated for several days and can also be used to make dosa or uttapam.
- A wet grinder generally produces especially soft idlis, but a strong blender also works.
